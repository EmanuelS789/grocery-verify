GroceryScouter Data Sources

Last updated: September 21, 2026

1. Open Food Facts (ODbL)

Some product names, brands, categories and package sizes shown in GroceryScouter come from Open Food Facts, the open food products database. That data is made available under the Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0. Open Food Facts is not affiliated with GroceryScouter and does not endorse it.

Where a product’s details came from Open Food Facts, the app shows a “Product details from Open Food Facts” link on that product’s page that opens the matching Open Food Facts product page.

2. What we derive, and under which licence

The product-identity records we build from Open Food Facts data (names, brands, categories and sizes, keyed by barcode), together with any corrections our users make to them, form a database derived from Open Food Facts. We make that derived part available under the same Open Database License, as the licence requires.

Everything else in GroceryScouter — prices, price history, stores, user accounts, shopping lists, price alerts and the app’s own code — is our own or our users’ work and is not derived from Open Food Facts. It is not covered by this notice.

3. Cleaning scripts and corrections

Our cleaning scripts and a corrections CSV are published on this page. They are periodically updated, and they are also available on request at groceryscoutersupport@gmail.com. The corrections export carries product facts only — barcode, name, brand, category and size — and never any user identifier or per-user timestamp.

  • Cleaning scripts — README.md (what is kept, what is dropped, and the run order), extract.py (the Open Food Facts Parquet export → JSONL), clean.ts with normalize.ts and jsonl.ts (JSONL → the load file), probe.py (the survey of the export that chose the filters) and deno.json. The scripts call the app’s own barcode normaliser, package-size grammar and category mapper, which are part of the app’s source rather than of this page; that source is available on request. The current load is described in report.md: the Open Food Facts Parquet export of September 15, 2026 — 1,315,018 rows read, 1,085,050 kept.
  • Corrections CSV — The corrections export follows the first production load. A row will be a product whose details as stored in GroceryScouter differ from the current Open Food Facts record: the barcode, then the stored and the Open Food Facts name, brand, category (as mapped by our category rules) and package size, side by side.

4. USDA Global Branded Food Products Database (CC0)

When Open Food Facts does not know a barcode, GroceryScouter next checks a second, smaller reference table built from the USDA FoodData Central Branded Foods release (the Global Branded Food Products Database), which USDA publishes into the public domain under CC0 1.0. CC0 carries no licence obligations — no attribution is required — and the app shows a one-line courtesy notice in Settings › Legal. The USDA data is kept in its own table, separate from the Open Food Facts data, and the two are never merged or written into each other. Products identified from it are marked as such internally and carry no Open Food Facts link.

  • Cleaning scripts — README.md (what is kept, what is dropped, and the run order), extract.py (the USDA CSV release → JSONL), clean.ts with normalize.ts (JSONL → the load file; they share the JSONL reader, CSV encoder and dedupe published under off-import/), probe.py with probe_rules.ts (the survey of the release that chose the filters), and the two reviewed inputs the cleaner reads: usda_category_map.csv (USDA category → our category) and usda_retailer_owners.csv (the retailer-owner list behind the store-brand flag). As with the Open Food Facts scripts, the app’s own barcode normaliser, package-size grammar and store-brand list are part of the app’s source, available on request. The current load is described in report.md: the FoodData Central Branded Foods release of April 30, 2026 — 463,923 US-market rows read, 425,222 kept.
  • No corrections export — CC0 asks for none, and none is produced.

5. OpenStreetMap

The nearby-stores map is drawn with OpenStreetMap map tiles. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License. If something on the map is wrong, you can report a map issue to OpenStreetMap directly.
